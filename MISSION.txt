2. THE CORE AUTHORING PRINCIPLE: REASONING-FIRST, NOT TEMPLATE-FIRST
======================================================================

Do not create a large corpus by repeatedly substituting names, integers,
objects, or wording into a handful of fixed question templates.

Do not equate paraphrases, repeated trajectories, or alternate exports
with independent problems.

You may use deterministic code for:
- Simulators and executable task semantics.
- Arithmetic, logic, state, and constraint verification.
- Data conversion, hashing, deduplication, and split enforcement.
- Rendering reviewed structured cases into training formats.
- Carefully designed parameter variation within meaningful task families.

However, a fixed generator must not become the intellectual ceiling of
the corpus.

Use strong model reasoning or expert-style authoring to:
- Identify the capability being taught.
- Design a coherent situation with meaningful uncertainty.
- Develop plausible competing hypotheses or solution approaches.
- Determine what evidence would discriminate between them.
- Construct correct, incorrect, ambiguous, and boundary cases.
- Write clear solutions and reusable principles.
- Identify when a variation changes the reasoning, not merely the words.

Use an adaptive authoring loop:
coverage gap → evidence or scenario design → independent solution →
verification → adversarial review → revision or rejection → admission.

Inspect emerging repetition and blind spots after every batch. Expand
the authoring strategies when necessary. Do not keep scaling a narrow
recipe simply because it is easy to automate.

Preserve stable, versioned semantics within an accepted release.
Adapt authoring between recorded batches; do not silently mutate
accepted data or evaluators.

3. DATA RIGHTS: STRICT ADMISSION, NOT LICENSE GUESSING
======================================================================

The owner requests data without restrictive reuse licensing.

Default external-content admission:
A. CC0 material with a verified applicable dedication.
B. Material affirmatively documented as public domain, with jurisdiction
   and reuse limitations recorded.
C. Newly authored material whose inputs and generation-service terms
   permit the intended training, modification, commercial use, and
   redistribution.

Do not treat "open source," "open access," "free download," a repository
license, or a Hugging Face license tag as sufficient proof of data rights.

Do not silently admit attribution-required, share-alike, noncommercial,
research-only, no-derivatives, model-training-restricted, or unknown-license
content into the unrestricted corpus.

A source-code license does not automatically license bundled text,
problems, solutions, images, or third-party datasets.

For every external source, retain:
- Original source and precise artifact/version.
- Publisher or rights holder.
- License/dedication evidence and retrieval date.
- Applicable scope, including component-specific exclusions.
- Content hash where available.
- Original dataset split.
- Permitted transformations and redistribution.
- Relevant provider/API restrictions.
- Admission decision and reviewer identity.

Quarantine ambiguous sources. Do not rewrite restricted material and
claim that paraphrasing removed its restrictions.

Useful starting points for investigation, not blanket approvals:
- Wikidata structured-data namespaces covered by CC0.
- Specifically verified public-domain government scientific data.
- Other independently verified CC0 scientific or educational resources.

Do not assume Wikipedia article prose has Wikidata's license.
Do not assume every item hosted on a government domain is public domain.
Do not assume U.S. public-domain status proves worldwide status.

Keep research-paper reading separate from training-content admission.

For newly authored material, document generation provenance and the
applicable service/model terms. Do not declare ownership, legal clearance,
or a CC0 dedication that you lack authority to grant. Supply a proposed
release-rights record for owner review.

Preserve provenance even when attribution is not legally required.

4. BUILD RESEARCH EPISODES, NOT JUST ISOLATED ANSWERS
======================================================================

Use a canonical, evidence-backed parent record as the unit of authoring.

Depending on the task, a parent may contain:
- A source snapshot or authored world.
- A question or decision problem.
- Initial observations.
- Available actions and observation rules.
- Competing explanations.
- An independently established solution or outcome.
- Assumptions, applicability limits, and counterexamples.
- A verified concise answer.
- A concise educational derivation or proof certificate where useful.
- Source, dependency, review, and verification records.

Derive only useful views from that parent. Possible views include:
- Direct answer.
- Evidence-grounded answer.
- Hypothesis proposal.
- Discriminating-experiment selection.
- Hypothesis revision after new evidence.
- Counterexample identification.
- Concept or procedure abstraction.
- Memory applicability and reconstruction.
- Error correction.
- Compositional application.
- Teacher-context/student-query pairing.

Do not force every parent into every format.

Preserve which information was available at each decision.
A final solution must not leak into an earlier observation or student
prompt unless the declared task intentionally supplies it.

Label fictional worlds as fictional. Their rules can establish truth
inside the world, not new facts about real science.

5. COVER THE CAPABILITIES THE PROGRAM ACTUALLY NEEDS
======================================================================

Develop a balanced research portfolio across the following areas.
Choose proportions through documented coverage and quality analysis,
not an arbitrary equal-quota rule.

A. Concept and rule discovery
Provide multiple observations, competing hypotheses, legal experiments,
scope-aware conclusions, and counterexamples.

Include underdetermined cases where several rules fit the observations.
Reward a useful additional experiment or qualified conclusion rather
than inventing a uniquely established law.

Do not reduce the whole discovery corpus to polynomial fitting.

B. Source-grounded knowledge acquisition
Use admitted sources to author meaningful questions requiring:
- Accurate factual understanding.
- Relations between facts.
- Comparison and explanation.
- Multi-evidence integration.
- Recognition of insufficient support.
- Explicit temporal or scope qualifications.

Do not turn every passage into trivial span extraction.

C. Transfer and composition
Teach component concepts, then create genuinely different applications
and tasks requiring multiple operations.

Record the underlying reasoning structure, prerequisites, and operation
dependencies. Separate coexistence of skills from successful composition
within one problem.

D. Experience abstraction and reconstruction
Provide completed, verifiable episodes from which useful principles can
be extracted.

Include source conditions, supporting and failing examples, and limits.
Create new states where the principle should be used, adapted, rejected,
or superseded.

A principle must do more than restate the previous answer.

E. State tracking, tools, and bounded planning
Use authored event histories, API specifications, constrained actions,
resource budgets, and recoverable failures.

Verify semantic correctness, not merely valid JSON.
Use contained environments rather than unrestricted shell execution.

F. Error diagnosis and correction
Include realistic incorrect reasoning, subtle scope errors, misleading
evidence, and plausible but wrong solutions.

Construct mistakes deliberately or collect genuine attempts.
Label the origin accurately. Do not call authored mistakes "Rineya
failures."

G. Retention and natural communication
Preserve useful short-response behavior, factual adequacy, context
consistency, clarification, and calibrated uncertainty.

Use existing legacy replay material only when it is authorized and its
rights and split provenance are established. Newly authored preservation
examples are not evidence that historical Rineya capabilities were retained.

H. Sequential learning
Organize distinct, related, conflicting, and compositional learning units.
Support different registered learning orders without changing the
underlying evidence inventory.

Keep high-stakes medical/legal advice and unrestricted real-world agents
outside the first release.

6. BUILD THE PAIRING ENGINE AROUND SEMANTIC COMPATIBILITY
======================================================================

Implement an inspectable pairing and export engine.

Pair records because they share relevant concepts, task semantics,
prerequisites, evidence scope, or an experimental purpose—not simply
because an embedding similarity score is high.

For each pairing, record its rationale and compatibility checks.

Support these outputs:

1. Ordinary supervised examples
Verified student prompt and target, with appropriate response masks.

2. Verified-response training
A parent plus candidate attempts, acceptance/rejection evidence, and
accepted response targets.

When candidate attempts were not actually collected, label the material
as authored demonstrations, not empirical verified self-training.

3. Privileged-context distillation packets
Separate:
- Student-visible task and evidence.
- Teacher-only demonstration or completed experience.
- Verification evidence.
- Future rollout/scoring requirements.

A training teacher may intentionally receive a solution demonstration.
That does not authorize putting it in the corresponding unassisted
student input or a protected evaluation prompt.

4. Experience-to-principle and reconstruction pairs
Link source episodes to a scoped principle and link that principle to
new applicability decisions.

5. Preference pairs, where a real preference exists
Use plausible alternatives with an evidence-based reason for preference.
Do not label two valid solutions as chosen/rejected arbitrarily.
Do not manufacture preferences solely from verbosity or surface polish.

Keep incorrect answers out of positive SFT targets unless the response
explicitly identifies and corrects the error.

6. Retention replay manifests
Preserve task categories, source identities, token exposure, and allowed
reuse. Do not invent source-correct membership without model observations.

7. Interactive environment and collection contracts
Supply task initialization, legal actions, observations, verifier hooks,
and event logging for future student-generated trajectories.

A static offline corpus is not an on-policy dataset.
Never fabricate student prefixes, model probabilities, teacher logits,
success rates, or learning-progress labels.

At actual collection time, record checkpoint hashes, tokenizer identity,
generation policy, timestamps, and prefix provenance. Align teacher and
student scoring on identical response-token prefixes, not absolute
positions in differently sized prompts.

Frozen-versus-EMA teacher behavior belongs to the experimental learner.
Do not pretend that a static dataset determines this treatment.

7. REVIEW CONTENT INDEPENDENTLY AND RECORD THE EVIDENCE
======================================================================

Every admitted training target must receive:
- Structural validation.
- Provenance and rights checks.
- Task/answer compatibility checks.
- A content-verification or substantive review pass.

Use a separate solver/reviewer context where available. Have the reviewer
solve the problem before seeing the proposed answer when practical.

Repeated agreement from the same model is not independent ground truth.
Record whether review used a separate context, model, tool, or person.

Verification should match the task:
- Arithmetic: exact or justified numerical checks.
- Logic: entailment, satisfiability, or proof checks.
- Simulators: actual execution and fresh allowed witness cases.
- Code: sandboxed behavioral tests, including edge cases.
- Facts: traceable source support and scope.
- Open conversation: a calibrated semantic rubric and valid alternatives.

Do not convert model-judge approval into a "deterministically verified"
label.

Challenge:
- Ambiguity and unstated assumptions.
- Numerical coincidences.
- Unsupported causal explanations.
- Correct answers backed by wrong derivations.
- Missing boundary conditions.
- Incorrect applicability.
- Shortcut cues in wording, IDs, or metadata.
- Artificially easy negative examples.
- A verifier that shares the author's implementation bug.

Where possible, use separately implemented checks or counterexamples.

Record rejected items and reasons. Preserve attempted-generation and
review costs, not only accepted material.

Provide a stratified human-review packet. Unless a human actually reviews
it, record human review as pending. Do not fabricate a signature.

8. STRICT LINEAGE, SPLIT, AND TEMPORAL CONTROLS
======================================================================

Preserve the bundle's roles:
TRAIN, REPLAY, DEV, CONFIRMATION, SEALED.

Assign canonical worlds, source families, and question families before
creating derivatives. Keep related paraphrases, negatives, rationales,
translations, and episodes within their assigned experimental boundary.

Use more than string hashes:
- Canonical parent identity.
- Source-document and fact lineage.
- Structural reasoning signatures.
- Exact and normalized deduplication.
- Semantic-overlap screening with reviewed suspicious matches.

Do not rename a parent or source to bypass the existing checks.

Distinguish two claims explicitly:
A. Taught-fact consolidation: facts were intentionally taught; new question
   forms and applications test retention and use.
B. Untaught-source transfer: entire sources/families were withheld; any
   evidence supplied at inference is declared.

Any deliberate shared factual evidence in track A must be documented as
allowed learning exposure, not advertised as source-disjoint discovery.
If the current schema/firewall cannot express this distinction safely,
propose a versioned policy/schema amendment. Do not bypass it.

Do not read, copy, paraphrase, or reconstruct existing protected Rineya
benchmarks. Preserve inherited quarantines and "not for training" labels.

Do not use protected answers, evaluator feedback, or their descendants
in training, memory, teacher contexts, or curriculum decisions.

Use an authorized evaluator-side contamination check where available;
do not expose sealed content to the data author merely to deduplicate it.

Authoring a file and naming it "sealed" does not make it blind.
Create genuinely protected confirmation/final banks through a separate
evaluator workflow. Otherwise label them holdout candidates with author
exposure disclosed and reserve fresh independent confirmation for later.

For online episodes:
score the attempt first → finish the episode → verify the outcome →
admit eligible experience into a later learning cohort.

Future observations must never leak backward into a previously scored
decision.

9. DIVERSITY, DIFFICULTY, AND DATA SCALE
======================================================================

Measure diversity in reasoning requirements, not just words or topics.

Track:
- Independent parent and source-family counts.
- Reasoning structures and concept dependencies.
- Number and type of required operations.
- Evidence sufficiency and ambiguity.
- Distractors, exceptions, contradictions, and counterfactual changes.
- Context length and information location.
- Valid answer forms and linguistic variation.
- Source/authoring-strategy concentration.
- Variants per parent and duplicate clusters.

Inspect the largest families and repeated answer patterns.
Do not let one convenient source or simulator dominate unnoticed.

Create prerequisite-to-advanced difficulty ladders.
Store authored difficulty estimates separately from measured
Rineya success rates.

Only authorized, genuine model inference can provide empirical difficulty.
When unavailable, leave empirical fields unmeasured.

Use staged scale targets as planning bands, not quality-defeating quotas:

- Reviewed design sample: approximately 300–500 independent parents.
- Pilot corpus: approximately 2,000–5,000 independent TRAIN parents.
- Research v1: approximately 20,000–40,000 independent TRAIN parents,
  potentially yielding 60,000–120,000 useful training views.

Revise these bands using actual review cost, diversity, token budgets,
and coverage. Do not inflate counts to meet them.

Count parent problems, derived views, episodes, preference pairs, and
supervised tokens separately. A pair is not two independent problems.

Provide nested, reproducible subset manifests for economical pilot runs.
Size evaluation banks from the registered statistical questions rather
than automatically assigning a fixed train/test percentage.

A smaller admitted release is preferable to a larger unreliable release,
but clearly report any shortfall and preserve resumable progress.
