== [0]/summary 23.6
e-tuned GPT-3 alone reached only 20% on PlanBench. A fair amortization claim must count proposer tokens as well as world-model calls in one FLOPs currency, and compare success-vs-compute curves against uniform-policy search and BFS at matched compute, plus training compute and break-even task counts. BFS under F may already be cheaper in tokens on RW2. PlanBench's 3–5-block state spaces are also tiny: by my derivation from OEIS A000262, at most about 866 states for 5 blocks. The convincing amortization test is therefore on larger instances, such as the 6–20-block set where o1-preview drops to 23.6%.

== [0]/recommendations[4]/why 23.6
e-to-pretraining ratio. 'AI Agents That Matter' shows accuracy-only reporting misattributes gains. Thought of Search shows LLM-in-the-loop search can cost about 482K calls on Blocksworld vs about 4. In RW2, A00's K=4 full-plan generations per round may already cost more tokens than BFS's ~80 short memoized F calls, so the current 'F calls per task: 0' column understates proposer cost. On PlanBench 3–5 blocks, the state space is at most about 866 states, so exhaustive search under an accurate F would make 'rivals o1 (587/600)' trivial and unconvincing. The 6–20-block set, where o1-preview gets 23.63% at about $42 per 100 instances, is where amortization can show a non-trivial advantage.
