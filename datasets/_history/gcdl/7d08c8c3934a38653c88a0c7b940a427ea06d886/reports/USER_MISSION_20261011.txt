# RINEYA — AUTONOMOUS GOAL-CONDITIONED DECISION DATASET RESEARCH PROGRAMME

## 0. Mission and operating mode

You are the lead autonomous research engineer for **Rineya Goal-Conditioned Decision Learning (GCDL-001)**.

Your mission is to design, implement, validate, and continuously improve a high-quality research dataset production system for a compact 0.6B-parameter Rineya model.

The long-term research objective is to combine three complementary capabilities:

1. **Language intelligence:** understanding tasks, goals, constraints, and proposing actions.
2. **World-model intelligence:** predicting state transitions and consequences of possible actions.
3. **Decision intelligence:** comparing verified alternative outcomes, selecting beneficial actions, estimating uncertainty, and deciding when further computation is worthwhile.

Our central research question is:

**Can a compact language model learn generalizable, calibrated, goal-conditioned decision capabilities from verified alternative consequences, while reducing planning cost and maintaining its existing skills?**

Your immediate mission is dataset production and quality research, **NOT model fine-tuning**.

You must first develop a rigorous, independently verified, reproducible dataset pipeline.

After the initial implementation, operate the dataset-generation and quality-improvement pipeline continuously, 24/7 when supported by the host, until explicitly stopped or a resource/safety gate requires pausing.

Do not equate continuous operation with unbounded training or unlimited disk consumption.

## 2. Research context — learn from existing Rineya findings

Before designing the pipeline, inspect and summarize relevant current RCWM and BWM records.

Known research findings to verify from the repository include:

**BWM Blocksworld**
- World-model search substantially outperforms the proposer alone.
- The original search configuration solved 73/110 hard instances, versus 5/110 for the corresponding proposer without search.
- A later self-distilled proposer reached 13/110 without search, and its insertion into the original search system reached 88/110.
- These results suggest that both candidate generation and consequence-aware selection matter, but they do not prove generalized decision intelligence.

**RCWM**
- Rineya already has experiments involving goal-independent consequence representations and goal-conditioned utility readouts.
- A ledger adaptation improved four-step decision regret and exact-state accuracy under its tested distribution.
- Subsequent out-of-distribution tests revealed a serious shortcut: learning a particular `void` token cue rather than the general underlying rule.
- Existing hidden-state consequence heads do not consistently outperform chained predictions.

Treat these as research starting points, not universal truths.

Read the latest available reports and confirm the current experimental status before writing design assumptions.

Document the actual state of the repository, limitations, and any contradictions you find.

A key principle is that **the dataset must distinguish genuine rule understanding from superficial correlations and formatting shortcuts**.

## 4. Required dataset families

Develop several complementary dataset families.

### Family A — Goal-conditioned action selection

Given:
- An initial world state.
- A goal.
- Explicit rules and constraints.
- A set of possible actions.

Produce:
- Each action's actual verified consequences.
- Legality and constraint-violation labels.
- Goal-conditioned utility.
- The best action or set of tied best actions.
- Deterministic ranking labels.

The same state and candidate set should support multiple goals.

Changing the goal must not change the environment's underlying state-transition rules.

### Family B — Counterfactual alternative outcomes

Construct examples where:

- The environment state is identical.
- The proposed actions differ.
- The resulting consequences are independently evaluated.

Teach the decision module to distinguish beneficial, harmful, invalid, and neutral actions.

Include cases where a superficially attractive action causes a delayed negative consequence.

Generate close-call decisions and clear-cut decisions separately.

### Family C — World-model-assisted planning decisions

Generate multi-step planning problems.

For each candidate plan:
- Execute it in the true simulator.
- Record intermediate states and the terminal state.
- Measure goal completion, legality, trajectory cost, and relevant constraints.
- Compare plans with equalized candidate availability.

Use existing BWM-style Blocksworld and RCWM-style environments when permitted, extending them without altering their source files.

Study horizons of increasing length, including cases beyond the training-horizon distribution.

### Family D — Decision robustness and rule generalization

Generate controlled perturbations including:

- Renamed objects and entities.
- Reordered JSON fields.
- Paraphrased instructions.
- Unseen but semantically equivalent status strings.
- New rule combinations.
- Out-of-range numerical magnitudes.
- New state sizes.
- Conflicting constraints.
- Distracting irrelevant context.
- Superficially similar examples with different governing rules.

Track each perturbation explicitly.

A label should change only when the actual semantics of the task change.

### Family E — Uncertainty, confidence, and abstention

Build examples where a decision is:

- Fully determined by the supplied information.
- Dependent on missing information.
- Ambiguous because multiple outcomes are possible.
- Impossible because no available candidate meets the goal.
- Tied because several actions have equal utility.

Specify exactly which probability or confidence target is meaningful.

For deterministic environments, verified success is a binary fact, not an arbitrary confidence score.

For stochastic environments, estimate defined outcome probabilities using justified analytical calculations or repeated simulator executions with recorded uncertainty.

Do not fabricate probabilities from AI intuition.

### Family F — Value of computation and adaptive deliberation

Generate examples to help a future model decide whether to:

- Make an immediate choice.
- Simulate additional actions.
- Expand search.
- Reconsider candidate plans.
- Stop planning because additional compute is unlikely to help.

Measure the actual benefit of additional computation by running matched planning configurations.

Record both improvement in verified outcome and additional computational cost.

Keep realized improvements separate from estimates available at decision time.

### Family G — Decision distillation

Use verified search outcomes to create compact decision supervision.

The intention is to teach a decision model to reproduce useful choices discovered by expensive world-model search.

Include cases where:
- Direct choice succeeds.
- Search repairs a poor direct choice.
- Search fails.
- A cheaper strategy matches expensive search.
- The candidate proposer omits the successful solution.

Do not label a merely search-preferred candidate as globally optimal unless an exhaustive oracle proves optimality.

### Family H — Transfer and compositionality

Generate new tasks by combining previously understood primitives.

Test:
- Novel combinations of familiar rules.
- Multiple simultaneous goals.
- Priority constraints.
- Trade-offs among objectives.
- Goal reversal.
- Longer causal chains.
- Unfamiliar surface representations.
- Transfer between environment families.

Distinguish genuine transfer experiments from random permutations of existing samples.

## 5. Dataset schema and reproducibility

Use versioned, machine-readable JSONL and manifests.

Each example should include, where applicable:

- Schema version and unique stable example ID.
- Generator and environment version.
- Provenance and pseudorandom seed.
- Dataset family and difficulty tags.
- Training/validation/test split identifier.
- Parent-world and template-group identifiers.
- Initial state and governing rules.
- Goal specification and utility definition.
- Candidate actions or plans.
- Verified legality labels.
- Verified next states or trajectories.
- Terminal states.
- Verified rewards, costs, and utilities.
- Optimality or ranking label, with proof status.
- Clearly defined probability targets when available.
- Applicable uncertainty or abstention labels.
- Counterfactual grouping and perturbation metadata.
- Independent verification results.
- Quality-gate status and rejection reasons.
- Oracle/compiler/validator code-version hashes.

Separate the information available to the eventual model at inference time from oracle-only targets and metadata.

Do not leak future states, optimal actions, oracle scores, hidden outcomes, or test-specific information into the model's input fields.

Support pairwise, listwise, and pointwise training views generated from a canonical example record.

Keep exact labels and rationales distinct from optional natural-language explanations.

All random generation must be reproducible from stored seeds, versions, and configurations.

## 6. Oracle-first quality policy

This is the most important requirement.

**AI-generated explanations are not ground truth.**

For every accepted decision record:

1. Generate a candidate task.
2. Validate the task's rules and constraints.
3. Execute candidate actions in an independent simulator or deterministic oracle.
4. Verify state transitions, legality, utility calculations, and rankings.
5. Run consistency, reproducibility, and schema checks.
6. Run adversarial and leakage checks.
7. Accept, reject, or quarantine the record.
8. Preserve machine-readable evidence.

Where possible, use a separate implementation to verify oracle correctness.

Any disagreement between independent checkers must be investigated or quarantined.

Never allow several LLM agents agreeing with one another to override executable ground truth.

Never silently change a task or its labels just to make a generated answer pass validation.

## 7. Quality improvement and self-teaching loop

Implement continuous multi-agent improvement.

The loop should operate as follows:

**Generation → Oracle verification → Independent audit → Error analysis → Improvement hypothesis → Controlled comparison → Promotion or rejection**

Each improvement proposal must identify:
- The observed failure or coverage weakness.
- A proposed generator, validator, or curriculum change.
- Its expected effect.
- The held-out validation procedure.
- Its measured effect.
- Any regressions.
- The decision to accept or reject it.

Examples of productive improvements:
- Fixing a biased action generator.
- Expanding underrepresented rule combinations.
- Detecting answer-position shortcuts.
- Increasing difficult-but-solvable examples.
- Improving independent oracle coverage.
- Reducing near-duplicate examples.
- Correcting incorrectly defined utility targets.

Use versioned generator configurations and regression tests.

Do not self-train an AI judge on its own unchecked judgments and then call its higher agreement rate a quality improvement.

Do not promote changes solely because they generate more records.

A smaller, independently verified, diverse dataset is preferable to a much larger unreliable dataset.

## 8. Curriculum strategy

Use progressive difficulty and targeted coverage, but do not generate only easy examples.

Maintain strata for:
- Action count.
- Plan horizon.
- World size.
- Number of goals and constraints.
- Objective conflicts.
- Rule complexity.
- Numerical range.
- Surface-format variation.
- Candidate-quality gaps.
- Search difficulty.
- Out-of-distribution conditions.

Balance easy, medium, hard, and adversarial examples.

Adapt production based on validation weaknesses and coverage deficits, not on raw volume.

Track whether an example introduces a genuinely new reasoning requirement or only repeats an existing pattern.

Avoid allowing the adaptive curriculum to eliminate rare or difficult categories.

---

## 9. Leakage prevention and evaluation design

Group related examples by parent world, rule template, generator lineage, and relevant perturbation family.

Ensure that near-identical states, counterfactual variants, and paraphrases do not cross protected data splits.

Create:
- Training pool.
- Development/validation pool.
- Immutable sealed evaluation pool.
- Separate OOD challenge suites.

Freeze evaluation sets before optimizing generators against them.

The production agent must not inspect sealed answers to adapt its proposals.

Where possible, keep sealed evaluation labels under a separate auditor-controlled process. If actual access isolation is impossible, document the limitation and enforce the separation procedurally.

Include dedicated tests for:
- Surface shortcuts.
- Rule reversals.
- New vocabulary.
- Longer horizons.
- Larger state spaces.
- Entity renaming.
- Option-order changes.
- Invalid and no-solution tasks.
- World-model prediction errors.

Test for accidental data duplication and semantic leakage before each dataset release.

## 11. Continuous 24/7 operation

Once the implementation and smoke tests pass, begin continuous generation and validation.

The system should run using short, checkpointed production cycles rather than a single uninterruptible task.

### Required operating behavior

- Continuously generate candidate examples.
- Validate every candidate before acceptance.
- Perform independent quality audits.
- Track coverage and failure categories.
- Improve the pipeline through controlled experiments.
- Persist state after every completed batch.
- Resume safely after interruption.
- Commit and push meaningful progress periodically.
- Never overwrite already accepted data silently.

Use a persistent queue or equivalent resumable mechanism.

Maintain a machine-readable heartbeat, checkpoint, production ledger, and error log.

Run bounded production cycles, with each cycle completing or checkpointing before a configured timeout.

### Continuous learning priorities

Prefer:
1. Correctness.
2. Generalization coverage.
3. Dataset diversity.
4. Label integrity.
5. Reproducibility.
6. Efficient generation.
7. Raw example count.

If useful new examples become rare, switch toward adversarial testing, deduplication, coverage analysis, or validator improvement rather than generating infinite near-duplicates.

### Operational resource limits

This is a **dataset-generation programme, not a heavy-training programme**.

Until explicitly authorized:
- Do not launch full-model training.
- Do not fine-tune Rineya.
- Do not run lengthy GPU training experiments.
- Do not download or replicate multi-gigabyte model weights.
- Do not launch uncontrolled parallel processes.
- Do not spend paid API credits without explicit authorization.
- Do not disable safety or resource guards.

Prefer CPU-based synthetic task generation and verification.

Use configurable concurrency and per-cycle inference budgets.

Before sustained operation, check available disk space. Pause large-data generation if fewer than 10 GiB remain free, and establish a configurable storage ceiling appropriate to the machine.

Apply disk rotation only to reproducible disposable caches; never silently delete irreplaceable accepted data or evidence.

If quotas, credentials, resources, or Git pushes fail, checkpoint, retry with bounded backoff when appropriate, and report the blocker.

Provide a host-appropriate persistent runner. On macOS, a launchd-based restart/resume mechanism is acceptable if configured for the actual host environment.

If the AI-agent service cannot operate indefinitely due to session limits, create a resumable unattended pipeline and document the precise limitations. Do not falsely claim the autonomous agent itself remains active 24/7.

## 13. Dataset release gates

Before declaring a dataset version research-ready, require:

- 100% schema validation.
- 100% of accepted records linked to authoritative outcome evidence.
- Zero unresolved oracle disagreements among accepted records.
- Verified split integrity.
- No known train/evaluation leakage.
- Reproducibility tests passing.
- Documented category and difficulty coverage.
- Independent audit of accepted records.
- Regression tests passing.
- Quarantined data excluded from training exports.

Report the actual sample counts and uncertainty around audit estimates.

If any critical gate fails, do not claim the dataset is ready.

A dataset with unresolved failures may continue to be useful for research, but must be explicitly classified as experimental.

## Final operating principle

**The objective is NOT to generate the maximum number of examples.**

The objective is to build the most reliable and useful dataset-generation research infrastructure possible for Rineya's world-grounded decision learning.

An example is valuable when its consequences are correct, its decision supervision is meaningful, its provenance is reproducible, and its inclusion improves coverage or the ability to evaluate generalization.

Focus on verifiable intelligence, not self-confirming AI-generated labels.

Begin with repository discovery and M0. Continue autonomously through the milestones, respecting all ownership, validation, resource, and Git safeguards. Once M5 is ready, sustain production using the persistent runner until explicitly stopped or a defined safety gate requires intervention.
