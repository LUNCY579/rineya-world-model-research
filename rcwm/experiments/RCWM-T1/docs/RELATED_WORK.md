# RCWM-T1: relation to prior work

*Sources: three literature surveys run on 2026-10-06, covering (1) LLMs as world models, (2) probing and fine-tuning deltas, and (3) successor features. Only sources those surveys located are cited. Venues are omitted, and paper numbers are quoted only where a survey checked them. Every RCWM-T1 result below is an exploratory pilot result until PREREG-1 confirms it.*

## Relation to prior work

**One-step training, multi-step collapse and serial rollout (finding 1).** Writing out intermediate steps is well known to improve multi-step computation over answering directly ([Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114)). Theory says constant-depth transformers cannot do inherently serial computation, such as permutation composition, in a single pass, and that chain-of-thought steps add this ability ([Li, Liu, Zhou & Ma 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12875); [Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819)). The closest empirical analogue is [Balesni, Korbak & Evans 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16353). Their models were fine-tuned on facts presented separately and could not compose two of them without chain-of-thought, but could with it. A later version softens this for the case where one fact is pre-existing. Our decay, steeper than independent per-step errors predict, fits the "linearized subgraph matching" account of [Dziri et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18654), and [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06485) state the independent-error baseline explicitly. [Li, Guo & Andreas 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02854) show that the composition mechanism a model learns depends on its training tasks. One-step-only training may produce none. Our self-rollout is a scratchpad written outside the model. Feeding the model its own errors makes it drift, and the standard fix is to train on the model's own rollouts ([Venkatraman, Hebert & Bagnell 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9590)). We have not tried that fix, nor training on multi-step data with intermediate steps ([Lee et al. 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/6bf82fdcbd92b6a7793b3894422d2437-Abstract-Conference.html)).

**LM simulators for decisions (finding 2).** Rolling out an LM world model inside a planner is established ([Hao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14992); [Zhao, Lee & Hsu 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14078)), including with fine-tuned world models for web agents ([Chae et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13232); [Gu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06559)). Zhao et al. argue that an LM is better used as a world model than as a policy when the dynamics are simpler to describe, which matches our ordering. The web-agent papers find little gain beyond one step of lookahead because errors accumulate. We see gains at four steps, but in a small structured domain with exact simulators, so this does not contradict them. Fine-tuning 1.3B–13B models on simulator experience or world knowledge also improves planning ([Xiang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10626); [Qiao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14205)). One part of our result runs against prior work. In continuous control, successor-feature methods designed to avoid compounding rollout error beat model rollouts ([Zhu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06328); [Chen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17250)). In our pilot, serial rollout beats single-pass heads. The arms are not like-for-like: our heads read frozen hidden states with about 1.1k labels, while theirs learn representations from 2.5k–10k or more samples. Multi-step GPI ([Alegre et al. 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/77c7faab15002432ba1151e8d5cc389a-Abstract-Conference.html)) gives a frame: short rollouts should win when one-step model error is small compared with the error of the single-pass evaluator. A symbolic learner with 4,000 simulator labels wins on ledger. That echoes program-based world models ([Tang, Key & Ellis 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12275)) and the finding that the choice of features dominates performance ([Touati, Rapin & Ollivier 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14935)).

**Factorised vs. scalar goal evaluation (finding 4).** This is the expected successor-feature pattern. Splitting value into consequence features times linear goal weights gives exact evaluation of any linear goal for a fixed policy ([Barreto et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312)). Our 8 precommitted plans are such a fixed library. Scalar goal-conditioned evaluators interpolate between training goals but extrapolate poorly ([Schaul et al. 2015](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/schaul15.html)), and factorised approximators beat them on goals unseen in training ([Borsa et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07626)). The closest LM precedent is [Cao et al. 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/cao24a.html), which uses millions of labels. The caveat that successor features fail to transfer across optimal policies ([Lehnert, Tellex & Littman 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00102)) does not apply to fixed plans. On terms: in this literature, "span" usually means rewards that lie in the span of the features. Our "out-of-span" means goal weights outside the span of the scalar evaluator's training goals, with every goal linear in the features. Our features are simulator-defined, sidestepping feature learning.

**Probing (finding 3).** Entity state is known to be linearly decodable ([Li, Nye & Andreas 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00737)). So is the gap between next-step competence and a coherent world model ([Vafa et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03689)). Our selectivity of about 0 is read through control tasks ([Hewitt & Liang 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368)). The probe can compute the labels from surface features, so decodability is not evidence that the model represents them. The skill shows through the LM head but not through change-mask probes, the reverse of [Orgad et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02707). Othello-GPT's board state became linear only in the right basis ([Nanda, Lee & Wattenberg 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941)). A negative probe result is therefore weak evidence.

**The fine-tuning delta as an activation offset (finding 5).** The closest precedent is [Wang, Engels et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08218): a LoRA fine-tune "essentially adds a constant steering vector," and a vector trained from scratch reproduces the effect. [Minder et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13900) find that narrow fine-tunes, including on Qwen models, leave activation biases that can be read and used for steering. The "mode" framing fits work showing that fine-tuning reweights existing mechanisms ([Prakash et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811); [Jain et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12786)) and that one activation vector can carry a task ([Todd et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15213); [Hendel et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15916)). [Panigrahi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06600) show that a tiny sparse delta can keep most of a skill. A constant late-layer offset may be the echo of earlier changes that do depend on the input. The 1–3% residual therefore needs its own test, and the pending steering test is the causal check.

**Operational (finding 6).** No literature was searched, so we make no prior-work claim.

## What is and is not new here

The "plausibly new" items rest only on our searches not finding them, and those searches were not exhaustive. Nothing below should be called new until PREREG-1 confirms it.

1. **Finding 1, one-step skill, multi-step collapse and recovery by self-rollout: (a), a replication in a new setting.** Collapse without intermediate steps and recovery with them is known ([Balesni, Korbak & Evans 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16353); [Nye et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00114); [Li, Liu, Zhou & Ma 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12875)). Decay faster than independent errors predict also fits prior work ([Dziri et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18654)). Only the setting is new: 0.6B parameters, 384 examples, a sparse delta of 0.05% of the norm, and exact-match state transitions.
2. **Finding 2, serial rollout beats hidden-state heads and evaluators: mostly (a), with a narrow (b).** Planning with LM rollouts is known ([Hao et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14992); [Zhao, Lee & Hsu 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14078)). The plausibly new part is narrow: self-rollout compared with heads trained on the same frozen model's hidden states, at matched labels (about 1.1k). The ordering differs from continuous control ([Zhu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06328)), but the arms differ too, so do not claim a reversal. Absolute regret is still 0.39–0.51, and a symbolic learner with 4,000 labels beats the rollout on ledger.
3. **Finding 3, probes decode only about one step, with selectivity near 0: (a) for the result, (c) for the lesson.** The result is a negative probing result in a new model. The methodological lesson, that a probe without control tasks or in the wrong basis says little, is known ([Hewitt & Liang 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368); [Nanda, Lee & Wattenberg 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941)). The result does not show that the model has no multi-step representation.
4. **Finding 4, factorised head wins on out-of-span goals and ties in span: (c), already known.** See [Barreto et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312), [Borsa et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07626) and [Schaul et al. 2015](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/schaul15.html). The report already notes that this mostly holds by construction. The setting is new (a frozen LM's hidden states, about 1.1k matched labels), so present it as a confirmation.
5. **Finding 5, the delta acts as a near-constant per-task offset: (a), with a possible (b) pending.** Prior work already shows a constant-vector effect of fine-tuning ([Wang, Engels et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08218); [Minder et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13900)). What may be new is the measured 97–99% share for a skill that looks input-conditional. That stays unconfirmed until the steering test, and the offset may be a downstream echo of earlier changes.
6. **Finding 6, TDR resets under sustained FP32 load: not a research finding.** No literature was searched and nothing is cited. Report it as an engineering note, not a contribution.
