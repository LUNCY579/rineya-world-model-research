# S-roll on the pilot bank (EXPLORATORY): does Titan's chained rollout beat a generic one-step learner chained the same way?

**Status: exploratory, every number.** PREREG-1 sec 6 registered S-roll but did not implement it, so nothing here is confirmatory. The pilot TEST split was used heavily while this analysis was built: pre-grid feasibility fits printed TEST accuracy, three adversarial verifiers recomputed TEST contrasts, and the alternative learners below were added after their TEST numbers were seen. S-roll was not run on the sealed confirmation bank. TEST parents: L 26, P 28, R 26 (P paired n = 26: the frozen regret rule drops 2 P parents with a zero denominator). Generated by `scripts/s_roll.py` (+ `s_roll_report.py` for formatting); numbers are in `runs/bank_pilot/analysis/s_roll.json`.

- **Regret**: normalised k-step decision regret (0 = oracle, about 1 = random), computed by `decisions.g_only` itself: TEST goals seed 1001, `decide.regret_table`, a fresh rng 17 per method, parent-mean fill for unparseable states. S-roll's per-parent regret is the nanmean over its models.
- **S-roll**: an MLP on `decide.symbolic(fam, state, [event], kmax=1)` trained on gold one-step transitions of pilot TRAIN parents and chained 4 times on its own discrete predictions, exactly like Titan's chain. **FULL** = all TRAIN transitions (5 seeds). **TITAN-MATCHED** = 128 TRAIN transitions per family (equal row count, in-distribution; 5 resamples x 5 seeds). **TITAN-ROWS** = Titan's own 128 training rows per family (5 seeds).
- **Two selection rules**, each a complete set of arms; neither sees TEST. *spec*: head and size by DEV one-step exact accuracy (the S-roll spec). *DEV-regret*: by DEV k=4 regret under the selection goals (seed 1002), PREREG-1's rule for S.
- **Differences** are Titan − S-roll: **negative = Titan better**. 95% CIs: `decide.paired_bootstrap` over TEST parents.

## What this means

**L — Titan wins at matched data and loses at full data:** at 128 rows Titan chain_one (0.507) is better, Titan − S-roll = -0.185 [-0.317, -0.051] with random TRAIN rows and -0.158 [-0.305, -0.008] with Titan's own rows (DEV-regret selection: -0.20 [-0.33, -0.07] and -0.20 [-0.37, -0.02]), but at full data S-roll is better, +0.199 [+0.051, +0.345] with the spec-selected classifier and +0.456 [+0.332, +0.588] with the DEV-regret-selected regression head. With that regression head Titan's L lead is already gone at 512 TRAIN transitions (+0.053 [-0.076, +0.189]) and reversed at 2,000 (+0.373 [+0.244, +0.509]). **P — Titan never wins at k=4:** S-roll is better at full data (Titan − S-roll = +0.626 [+0.491, +0.766]) and with 128 random TRAIN rows (+0.333 [+0.220, +0.450]), and level with Titan (0.879) when trained on Titan's own 128 all-applicable rows (+0.127 [-0.025, +0.278]; Titan is better there only at k=1, -0.222 [-0.341, -0.097]). Titan's one-step P rule is itself wrong: 79 of 125 first steps on applicable actions are wrong (50 of them the integer sum s + add), it is right on 1 of 99 inapplicable ones, and only 59% of its P rollouts still parse at k=4. **R — Titan wins at matched and at full data:** Titan chain_one (0.386) is better than S-roll FULL (Titan − S-roll = -0.239 [-0.296, -0.182]) and than 128-row S-roll (-0.391 [-0.438, -0.342] with DEV-regret selection; -0.61 [-0.65, -0.56] under the spec rule, whose 128-row checkpoints stop at step 25 and nearly copy the input). The R gap is register `swap` (first-step accuracy Titan 1.000, S-roll FULL 0.000), which an MLP fed numbers as scaled scalars does not learn, while on `add` (0.85 vs 0.86) and `conditional` (0.45 vs 0.62) the full-data learner matches or beats Titan and both fail `mix`. So, relative to this generic chained learner, the LM adds something beyond "having a one-step simulator" on R (exact copying) and, at Titan's 128-row budget only, on L arithmetic, and nothing on P. All of this is exploratory: 26-28 pilot TEST parents per family, TEST seen during development, one learner family (no copy mechanism, no digit inputs), and S-roll's FULL and TITAN-MATCHED rows come from TEST's own generator while Titan's 384 rows do not.

## Design choices (made on DEV)

- **Outputs always decode to a valid discrete state.** P: 8 sigmoid bits, threshold 0.5. R: 97-way classification of the value (cls97) or of the residue (next − current) mod 97 (cls_res), a rounded regression mod 97 (reg_abs, reg_delta), or a gated delta (per-register change logit + regression). L: classification over the per-account deltas seen in TRAIN (cls_delta), rounded delta regression (reg_delta, Huber on delta/10), or the gated delta. R at FULL also screens a one-hot register-value input (sym1h).
- **Grid.** Stage A screens every head at 256 wide x 2 deep, LR 3e-3 (L: cls_delta, reg_delta, gated_delta; P: bits; R: cls97, cls_res, reg_abs, reg_delta, gated_delta); stage B tries (256, 2, 1e-3), (512, 2, 3e-3) and (256, 3, 3e-3) for the winner. Spec rule: DEV one-step exact (seed 17, resample 0). DEV-regret rule: DEV k=4 regret under selection goals 1002 (FULL: seed 17; 128 rows: mean of resamples 0-2), with a longer schedule for n > 1000 (≤ 8,000 steps, patience 20) because regression heads were still improving at the 4,000-step cap.
- **Optimiser.** AdamW (wd 1e-4), cosine decay to 5%, checkpoint = best DEV one-step exact under both rules. n > 1000: batch 256, ≤ 4,000 steps (spec), eval every 100, patience 12; n = 512: ≤ 3,000 steps; n = 128: full batch, ≤ 2,000 steps, eval every 25, patience 15. The 128-row arms also use pilot DEV (896-1,312 labelled transitions) for checkpointing, so their label budget is not strictly 128.
- **Not TEST-blind.** Nine pre-grid feasibility fits (R 7, L 2) printed TEST one-step accuracy; they led to the cls_res and gated_delta heads, the /10 Huber L regression and the sym1h screen. The DEV-regret rule and the extra arms were added after the verifiers reported TEST numbers. Within each rule every choice is made on DEV alone.

- **DEV-regret selection** added as a complete second set of arms (fairness verifier, must-fix), with a longer schedule for n > 1,000. - **TITAN-ROWS** added: S-roll trained on Titan's actual 128 rows per family. TITAN-MATCHED is relabelled "equal row count, in-distribution". ## Caveats

- **Exploratory, pilot bank only**, 26-28 TEST parents per family, TEST used repeatedly during development.
  Bootstrap CIs resample parents; they do not include learner-choice variability, which the sensitivity
  table shows is material for L at full data and R at 128 rows.
- **S-roll has a distribution advantage.** FULL and TITAN-MATCHED train on transitions from the pilot
  generator that also produced TEST, including void events and inapplicable actions. Titan's 384 rows came
  from the earlier Forge v2 distribution. TITAN-ROWS removes that advantage for the rows but keeps S-roll's
  access to pilot DEV for selection and checkpointing.
- **The budgets are not exactly matched.** Titan brings pre-training (number formats, copying, arithmetic);
  S-roll has only its rows plus DEV labels for checkpointing. FULL uses every intermediate transition of the
  pilot TRAIN chains, about 15-35 times Titan's 128 rows per family.
- **One generic learner family.** `decide.symbolic` feeds numbers as scaled scalars, so an MLP must reproduce
  products (L scale), copies (R swap) and modular sums (R mix) to integer precision. A learner with a copy
  mechanism or digit inputs would probably close most of the R gap; that was not tested.
- **The R capacity search did not converge.** With the spec configuration, DEV exact kept rising up to the
  depth-6 cap (0.29 → 0.41); that model gets R `add` 0.998 and `conditional` 0.897 teacher-forced but
  `swap` 0.029, and its k=4 regret (0.568) is still behind Titan. A larger MLP might narrow the R gap a little;
  removing it probably needs a learner that can copy values (untested).
- **Frozen float32 regret rule.** Some P parents have candidates that all reach one true φ; float32 rounding
  leaves a denominator of about 1e-6, so the frozen rule keeps some of them (each scores 0 for every method).
  The sensitivity table drops them without changing a conclusion. This also affects the PREREG-1 P analysis;
  the frozen code was not changed.
- **One-step exactness and regret do not map one to one.** A regression head that is approximately right on
  ledger `scale` has lower decision regret than a classifier that is exactly right more often but far off on
  `scale`; that is why the two selection rules differ on L.
